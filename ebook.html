Rose - Arshmi's Garden Diary
Rosa indica

Introduction

Rose, the queen of flowers, is cherished for its beauty and fragrance. It comes in a variety of colors and sizes, perfect for any garden.

Planting Tips

  • Plant in well-drained loamy soil.
  • Choose a sunny location (at least 6 hours of sunlight daily).
  • Space plants about 2–3 feet apart to ensure airflow.

care tips

  • Water deeply but infrequently, allowing the soil to dry slightly between waterings.
  • Prune regularly to promote fresh growth and more blooms.
  • Use organic fertilizers or rose-specific feeds every 2–4 weeks in growing season.
  • Protect from aphids and fungal diseases using neem oil or mild soap spray.

Facts about Rose:

  1. Rose is one of the oldest cultivated flowers — It has been grown for over 5,000 years.
  2. There are over 300 species and thousands of cultivars of roses.
  3. The rose is the national flower of several countries, including the United States, England, and Iran.
  4. Rose petals are edible and used in teas, desserts, and cosmetic products.
  5. The world’s most expensive rose took 15 years to breed and cost $5 million — it’s called the “Juliet Rose”.

Hibiscus

Hibiscus rosa-sinensis

All About Hibiscus

🪴 General Info:

Hibiscus is a stunning flowering plant known for its bold, colorful blooms and tropical appearance. It thrives in warm climates and can be grown in both pots and garden beds. The plant comes in many colors — red, pink, yellow, white, and orange.

🌿 Care Tips:

  • Soil: Well-draining, slightly acidic soil enriched with organic matter is best for hibiscus.
  • Watering: Keep the soil consistently moist but not soggy. Water more during summer and reduce in winter.
  • Sunlight: Needs 5–6 hours of full sunlight daily for best flowering.
  • Temperature: Thrives in warm climates; protect from frost and cold drafts.
  • Fertilizer: Use a balanced, water-soluble fertilizer every 2–3 weeks during the growing season.

✅ Fun Facts about Hibiscus:

  1. Hibiscus tea is popular for its tangy flavor and health benefits.
  2. It's the national flower of Malaysia (called "Bunga Raya").
  3. Hibiscus symbolizes beauty, fame, and delicate charm.
  4. Flowers are edible and often used in herbal medicine.
  5. The plant attracts pollinators like butterflies and hummingbirds.

Jasmine / Mogra

Jasminum sambac

All About Mogra

🪴 General Info:

Jasmine, also known as Mogra, is a beautifully fragrant flowering plant widely loved in India and many parts of the world. Its small white blooms are symbolic of purity and are often used in garlands, perfumes, and spiritual rituals.

🌿 Care Tips:

  • Soil: Well-draining sandy or loamy soil enriched with organic compost is best.
  • Watering: Keep the soil consistently moist but not soggy. Water more during the summer bloom period.
  • Sunlight: Requires full sunlight (5–6 hours daily) to bloom abundantly.
  • Temperature: Prefers warm temperatures between 20–30°C. Protect from frost.
  • Fertilizer: Use phosphorus-rich fertilizer monthly during the growing season.

✅ Fun Facts about Jasmine:

  1. Jasmine is the national flower of the Philippines and Indonesia.
  2. Mogra is often used in Ayurvedic oils and beauty products for its calming fragrance.
  3. It blooms best during the summer and early monsoon.
  4. Attracts beneficial pollinators like bees and butterflies.
  5. Jasmine tea is known to relieve stress and improve sleep quality.

Portulaca

All About Portulaca

Portulaca, commonly known as Moss Rose, is a low-growing flowering plant that thrives in hot, sunny locations. It is known for its vibrant blooms and succulent-like leaves, making it a popular choice for hanging baskets, borders, and pots.

Care Tips:

  • Soil: Well-draining sandy or loamy soil is ideal. Avoid heavy clay soils.
  • Watering: Water moderately. Allow the soil to dry out between waterings. Avoid overwatering.
  • Sunlight: Needs full sun (6–8 hours) daily to bloom vigorously.
  • Temperature: Thrives in warm climates and tolerates heat well. Sensitive to frost.
  • Fertilizer: Not a heavy feeder, but monthly liquid fertilizer during blooming helps.

Fun Facts:

  • Portulaca blooms close at night and on cloudy days — they love sunlight.
  • It's a succulent, which means it stores water in its leaves.
  • Very easy to propagate by stem cuttings or seeds.
  • Comes in a variety of colors including pink, yellow, red, and white.
  • Often called “10 o'clock flower” as it blooms late morning.

Ixora

Botanical name: Ixora coccinea

All about Ixora

Ixora is a tropical to semi-tropical evergreen shrub blooming with vibrant clusters of flowers in red, orange, pink, or yellow. Known for its ornamental beauty and ability to attract pollinators, it is a favorite among gardeners for borders and hedges. Ixora thrives in warm climates and blooms profusely in full sun.

Care Tips

Soil: Well-draining, slightly acidic soil is best.

Watering: Water regularly but avoid waterlogging.

Sunlight: Full sun to partial shade.

Temperature: Prefers warm, humid weather (20–32°C).

Fertilizer: Use balanced fertilizer once a month during growing season.

Fun Facts about Ixora

  • Ixora is sometimes called "Jungle Flame" due to its fiery-colored blooms.
  • In traditional medicine, it’s used for its anti-inflammatory properties.
  • Ixora is considered sacred in some cultures and is often used in temple offerings.
  • The plant can bloom year-round in tropical regions.

Tecoma

Botanical name: Tecoma stans

All about Tecoma

Tecoma is a fast-growing flowering shrub known for its bright trumpet-shaped blooms, often in shades of yellow or orange. It's a hardy plant that can tolerate heat and drought, making it a great choice for low-maintenance gardens. Tecoma attracts pollinators like bees, butterflies, and hummingbirds.

Care Tips

Soil: Well-draining sandy or loamy soil works best.

Watering: Water moderately; allow soil to dry slightly between waterings.

Sunlight: Full sun is essential for abundant flowering.

Temperature: Thrives in warm climates; frost-sensitive.

Fertilizer: Use a balanced liquid fertilizer once a month during active growth.

Fun Facts about Tecoma

  • Tecoma is also known as Yellow Bells or Trumpet Bush.
  • It’s native to the Americas but is now grown worldwide.
  • The plant can be shaped into a small tree or hedge.
  • Its bright flowers bloom continuously through warm seasons.

Succulent

Botanical name: Varies by type (e.g., Echeveria, Aloe, Sedum)

All about Succulent

Succulents are a diverse group of plants known for their thick, fleshy tissues that store water. They are ideal for low-maintenance gardens and indoor spaces due to their drought-tolerant nature. Available in a wide range of shapes and colors, succulents are perfect for decorative arrangements and terrariums.

Care Tips

Soil: Use well-draining cactus or succulent mix.

Watering: Water deeply but infrequently. Allow soil to dry completely between waterings.

Sunlight: Bright indirect sunlight is best; a few hours of morning sun is ideal.

Temperature: Prefers warm, dry conditions; protect from frost.

Fertilizer: Light feeding with diluted succulent fertilizer during the growing season.

Fun Facts about Succulent

  • Succulents store water in their leaves, stems, or roots.
  • There are over 10,000 known succulent species.
  • Some succulents like Aloe Vera have medicinal uses.
  • Succulents thrive on neglect — overwatering is their biggest threat.

Madhukamini

Murraya paniculata

All about Madhukamini

Madhukamini, also known as Orange Jasmine, is a fragrant evergreen shrub commonly found in Indian gardens. It blooms frequently with small white flowers that spread a sweet smell all around. Its dense green foliage and fast-growing nature make it a popular choice for hedges and decorative pots.

Care Tips

  • Soil: Well-drained fertile soil works best.
  • Watering: Water when the topsoil feels dry. Avoid waterlogging.
  • Sunlight: Full sun to partial shade is ideal.
  • Temperature: Prefers warm tropical climates.
  • Fertilizer: Use compost or balanced liquid fertilizer monthly.

Fun facts about Madhukamini

  • It is also called Orange Jasmine due to its flower fragrance.
  • Attracts butterflies and bees, making your garden more lively.
  • Often used as a natural green fence or boundary.
  • It is easy to shape through regular pruning.
  • Blooms several times a year with minimal care.

Rain Lily

Zephyranthes

All about Rain Lily

Rain Lily, scientifically known as Zephyranthes, is a charming bulbous plant that blooms after rainfall, hence the name. The flowers are delicate, usually in pink, white, or yellow, and appear almost magically after a good shower. It is a low-maintenance favorite in home gardens and balconies.

Care Tips

  • Soil: Loose, well-draining soil is ideal.
  • Watering: Water regularly, especially after dry spells.
  • Sunlight: Full sun to partial shade.
  • Temperature: Thrives in warm, humid climates.
  • Fertilizer: Feed with compost or bulb-boosting fertilizer every 4–6 weeks.

Fun facts about Rain Lily

  • Called “Rain Lily” because it blooms right after rainfall.
  • Blooms last only a few days but appear frequently throughout the season.
  • Can multiply easily through bulbs.
  • Very popular in both pots and ground planting.
  • The flowers close at night and open with sunlight.

Gold Dust Croton

Codiaeum variegatum 'Gold Dust'

All about Gold Dust Croton

Gold Dust Croton is a vibrant, eye-catching houseplant known for its glossy green leaves sprinkled with bright yellow specks—hence the name. Native to tropical Asia and the western Pacific, it's a favorite among gardeners and plant lovers for its striking foliage and low maintenance needs. It adds an exotic flair to any indoor or outdoor space.

Care Tips

  • Soil: Well-draining, fertile potting mix with peat or perlite.
  • Watering: Keep soil evenly moist; don’t let it dry out completely or stay soggy.
  • Sunlight: Bright, indirect sunlight. Can tolerate some direct morning sun.
  • Temperature: Prefers warm conditions (18°C–29°C). Keep away from cold drafts.
  • Fertilizer: Use a balanced liquid fertilizer every 4–6 weeks during growing season.

Fun Facts about Gold Dust Croton

  • Its spotted leaves resemble splashes of gold paint—hence the name "Gold Dust."
  • This plant is often used in Feng Shui to attract positivity and good luck.
  • Crotons are part of the Euphorbia family and contain a milky sap that can irritate skin—handle with care.
  • Gold Dust Croton can be trained into small shrubs or bonsai-style forms with pruning.
  • It’s known to be air-purifying and can improve indoor air quality slightly.

Fruits & Veggies

Pomegranate

Punica granatum

All about Pomegranate

Pomegranate is a fruit-bearing shrub or small tree known for its vibrant red fruits filled with juicy seeds. Native to the Middle East but widely cultivated in warm climates globally, it’s prized for both ornamental beauty and nutritious harvest.

Care Tips

  • Soil: Prefers well-draining loam or sandy soil with good organic content.
  • Watering: Water deeply but sparingly, allowing the soil to dry between watering.
  • Sunlight: Needs full sun (at least 6 hours per day) for good fruiting.
  • Temperature: Tolerates heat well; some varieties can survive mild frost.
  • Fertilizer: Feed monthly during growing season with balanced fertilizer or compost.

Interesting Info

  • Pomegranates are rich in antioxidants, vitamins, and have been used medicinally for centuries.
  • The wood and bark of the plant were used historically to create natural dyes.
  • It symbolizes prosperity and fertility in many cultures.

China Orange

Aegle marmelos

All about China Orange

China Orange, also known as Bael (Aegle marmelos), is a small tree with aromatic leaves and fruits often used in traditional medicine. Native to India and Southeast Asia, the fruit has a hard shell with sweet, fibrous pulp inside. It’s valued for its health benefits and religious significance in many cultures.

Care Tips

  • Soil: Well-draining loamy soil with good organic matter.
  • Watering: Water abundantly during flowering and fruiting season; reduce in winter.
  • Sunlight: Needs full sun exposure for healthy growth.
  • Temperature: Thrives in warm to tropical weather; tolerate moderate drought.
  • Fertilizer: Apply compost or balanced fertilizer quarterly during the growing season.

Interesting Info

  • China Orange is considered sacred in many spiritual practices and often planted near temples.
  • The fruit is known for its digestive and immune‑boosting properties.
  • Leaves and fruits are used in Ayurvedic medicines and home remedies.
  • The tree blossoms with aromatic flowers each season.
